MASAREN Platform Architecture

Operating principle: A sports profile is valuable only when identity, eligibility,
performance, evidence, rights, and context can be independently verified.
Abstract
This document defines a dedicated Sports Profiling capability for the MASAREN platform. It explains how ministries, federations, clubs, academies, schools, event organizers,
athletes, coaches, medical teams, scouts, sponsors, broadcasters, and fans can work from
a shared, permissioned record without collapsing their responsibilities into one database.
The capability turns verified sporting activity into an athlete passport, team and institution profiles, competition records, performance evidence, development plans, opportunity
matches, media packages, sponsor inventory, and impact reporting. Egyptian examples are
used throughout, including the Ministry of Youth and Sports, the Egyptian Football Association, Al Ahly Sporting Club, Zamalek Sporting Club, national federations, academies,
and illustrative profiles for Mohamed Salah, Ahmed Elgendy, Sara Ahmed, Farida Osman,
Nour El Sherbini, and Ali Farag. These examples demonstrate profile design; they do not
imply endorsement, platform participation, or transfer of oﬀicial data ownership.

1 Sports Profiling Mission
The market problem. Egyptian sport produces large volumes of valuable information, but
athlete identity, club registration, competition results, training evidence, medical restrictions,
safeguarding decisions, media rights, sponsorship commitments, and social-impact activity are
often held by different institutions in incompatible formats. This fragmentation makes it diﬀicult
for a young athlete to prove development, for a federation to reconcile eligibility, for a club to
discover talent, for a sponsor to verify exposure, or for a broadcaster to identify trustworthy
stories. MASAREN addresses the coordination problem by preserving authoritative ownership
while creating verified links among records.
Institutional setting. The model is suitable for public bodies such as the Egyptian Ministry of
Youth and Sports, governing organizations such as the Egyptian Football Association and sportspecific federations, multi-sport clubs such as Al Ahly and Zamalek, private academies, schools,
universities, training centers, leagues, national teams, and event organizers. The Ministry portal illustrates the public-sector coordination role, the Egyptian Football Association publishes
football regulations, clubs, competitions, and national-team information, and Al Ahly’s oﬀicial
platform represents a club with football, academy, multi-sport, membership, and media functions (Al Ahly Sporting Club, 2026a, 2026b; Egyptian Football Association, 2026; Egyptian
Ministry of Youth and Sports, 2026).
Target outcomes. The Sports Profiling capability must make five outcomes measurable. Athletes should control a portable record of verified development. Institutions should reduce duplicate registration and improve roster, eligibility, and safeguarding decisions. Coaches and
scouts should compare evidence without treating an opaque score as truth. Sponsors and media
partners should buy rights and exposure against auditable inventory. Public bodies should
evaluate participation, inclusion, facilities, pathways, and program impact without exposing
private health or child data.

2 Canonical Sports Profile
Profile identity. Each person receives a stable MASAREN athlete identifier linked to verified
identity, preferred public name, Arabic and English display names, date-of-birth assurance,
nationality or representation data, guardian relationship when required, and the identifiers
issued by authorized federations or clubs. Public pages never expose government identifiers,
precise home location, or private contact details. The profile distinguishes a natural person
from registrations, contracts, team memberships, and public personas so that a transfer or
retirement does not create a second identity.
Sport participation. A profile can contain multiple disciplines, classifications, positions,
teams, competition levels, and seasonal registrations. A footballer may have an academy registration, club roster position, national-team call-up, and commercial representation agreement;
a modern pentathlete may have discipline-specific training records and international competi-

3

tion credentials; a swimmer may have event-specific personal bests and qualification standards.
Each relationship has an issuer, validity period, evidence, and revocation state.
Performance record. Performance is represented as signed observations rather than manually
edited achievements. Competition organizers or federations issue results; approved timing,
scoring, or statistics providers issue event data; coaches issue assessments; athletes may submit
training evidence that remains clearly distinguished from oﬀicial competition results. Every
metric carries sport, event, unit, context, source, collection method, time, confidence, and
correction history.
Private welfare record. Medical status, injury information, rehabilitation, anti-doping data,
safeguarding cases, and psychological support are separate protected records. A public profile
may show only a safe availability statement such as “eligible,” “temporarily unavailable,” or
“clearance required.” WADA standards demonstrate the sensitivity of anti-doping and athlete
personal information, while IOC safeguarding guidance emphasizes institutional responsibility
for preventing and responding to interpersonal violence (International Olympic Committee,
2024; World Anti-Doping Agency, 2021a, 2021b).
Public showcase record. The athlete chooses which verified achievements, biographies, media, community contributions, sponsor relationships, and contact routes are public. The platform attaches the issuer and verification date to every public claim. A profile can therefore
say that a medal, result, ranking, or appearance is federation-verified, organizer-verified, clubconfirmed, athlete-submitted, or editorially produced rather than presenting all content with
equal authority.

3 Sports Architecture on MASAREN
Bounded contexts. The sports capability extends the existing MASAREN architecture rather
than creating a disconnected application. Identity owns people and consent; Sports Registry
owns sport-specific identifiers and licenses; Institution owns clubs, federations, academies, facilities, and memberships; Competition owns events, entries, fixtures, and oﬀicial results; Performance owns observations and derived metrics; Development owns plans and coach reviews;
Health and Safeguarding own restricted cases; Media and Rights own publishable assets and
usage permissions; Marketplace owns trials, scholarships, contracts, camps, and sponsorship
opportunities; Reputation produces explainable projections; and Reporting creates institution,
sponsor, and public-sector views.

4 Egyptian Athlete Showcase Examples
Mohamed Salah. A profile example for Mohamed Salah would present a verified identity,
national-team relationship, oﬀicial appearances and goals sourced from authorized competition
data, position and role history, selected media, charitable or community activity when verified,
languages, sponsor rights, and audience reach. FIFA’s oﬀicial material provides an authoritative
source for national-team and World Cup context (FIFA, 2026). MASAREN would not copy
unverified social-media statistics into the oﬀicial record; it would keep oﬀicial results, editorial
stories, sponsor inventory, and fan engagement as separate linked domains.
Ahmed Elgendy. Ahmed Elgendy’s profile would demonstrate a multi-discipline athlete
model. Modern pentathlon results can be decomposed into competition, round, discipline,
score, rank, qualification status, and medal evidence, while the public showcase explains the
overall achievement without exposing private training or health data. The oﬀicial Olympic athlete profile supplies a verifiable source for his Olympic participation and results (International
Olympic Committee, 2026a).
Sara Ahmed. Sara Ahmed’s example would use weight category, bodyweight at competition,
successful and unsuccessful attempts, snatch, clean and jerk, total, ranking, medal, and qualification cycle as structured evidence. Her Olympic profile can anchor verified Olympic claims,
while training-load, injury, and weight-management records remain restricted to the athlete
and authorized performance or medical teams (International Olympic Committee, 2026b).
Farida Osman. Farida Osman’s profile would organize oﬀicial swimming results by pool
length, stroke, distance, heat, reaction time, split, finish time, record status, competition, and
date. World Aquatics provides an oﬀicial results and athlete source that can be linked rather
than manually retyped (World Aquatics, 2026). MASAREN can add a verified media portfolio, clinics, appearances, and sponsor campaigns without mixing them into oﬀicial performance
statistics.
Nour El Sherbini and Ali Farag. Squash profiles would connect PSA identity and ranking
evidence, tournament draws, match results, games, points, opponent context, season summaries,
and approved highlights. The PSA rankings illustrate the authoritative tour source for players
such as Nour El Sherbini and Ali Farag (Professional Squash Association, 2026). A scouting
view could compare recent workload and opponent-adjusted form, but an explanation panel
must show that the comparison is a decision aid rather than an oﬀicial ranking replacement.

6

Youth athlete example. Consider a 15-year-old swimmer from an Alexandria club. The
guardian approves a private profile; the club verifies membership; the federation issues the
athlete identifier; oﬀicial meet results arrive from the organizer; the coach records technique
observations; the school verifies attendance support; and a sponsor sees only the public achievements and approved contact route. Precise health, school, and location data remain hidden.
When the athlete changes clubs, the profile preserves history, records the new registration, and
prevents either institution from rewriting prior results.

5 Institutional Operating Models
Ministry and national program. The Ministry of Youth and Sports can use aggregated,
privacy-preserving dashboards to understand participation by governorate, gender, age band,
discipline, facility, pathway stage, and program outcome. The Ministry should see program
effectiveness and verified facility utilization, not unrestricted athlete dossiers. Grants can be
linked to measurable outputs such as verified coaching hours, competitions delivered, athletes
retained, accessibility improvements, and safeguarding readiness.
Federation. The Egyptian Football Association or another national federation remains authoritative for licenses, competition rules, disciplinary status, oﬀicial fixtures, and results within its
remit. MASAREN provides registration workflow, data validation, institution interfaces, audit
history, and controlled public distribution. A club can submit a roster, but the federation signs
the eligible roster; a broadcaster can consume fixtures, but it cannot edit them; a player can
appeal a record, but the correction is issued by the owner.
Club. A multi-sport club such as Al Ahly or Zamalek can operate institution, branch, academy,
team, coach, facility, athlete, membership, competition, media, and sponsor views from one platform while preserving separate access domains. The football department may see contractual
and performance data; a swimming coach sees only authorized swimmers; the media team sees
approved public assets; the sponsor team sees inventory and delivery; and safeguarding oﬀicers
receive cases without sharing them with commercial staff.
Academy and school. Academies and schools can issue verified attendance, assessment, competition, and progression records. A parent can compare programs using coach credentials,
safeguarding readiness, facility evidence, verified alumni outcomes, and transparent fees rather
than promotional claims alone. Scouts can discover consented profiles that meet age, geography, position, performance, and availability criteria without receiving private school or family
information.

6 End-to-End Athlete Workflow
Onboarding. An athlete or guardian creates an identity, selects privacy preferences, and requests aﬀiliation with a club or federation. The institution verifies the relationship, Sports
Registry checks duplicates, and the authorized federation or competition body issues the rele-

7

vant registration. Safeguarding and consent rules determine who can see, contact, evaluate, or
publish information.
Development. A coach creates a development plan with measurable goals, training blocks,
review dates, and evidence expectations. Wearable or test data can be ingested only through approved sources and never treated as oﬀicial competition performance. The athlete sees progress
and explanations; the coach sees authorized training context; scouts see only consented, normalized summaries.
Competition. Competition Service receives the entry, verifies registration and clearance status, records the start list, and ingests oﬀicial results. A result is certified by the authorized
organizer or federation, stored with source and correction history, and projected into the athlete
passport. Notification informs the athlete and club, while Reporting updates team and program
outcomes.
Opportunity. Marketplace matches the verified profile to trials, scholarships, camps, club
needs, sponsor campaigns, media interviews, or community programs. Hard eligibility rules are
applied before ranking. The recommendation explains decisive factors, supports rejection and
appeal, and cannot use protected health, family, religion, or inferred socioeconomic traits.
Commercial and media use. When the athlete accepts a sponsor or media opportunity,
Rights Service records territory, channels, dates, asset types, exclusivity, approvals, and compensation terms. Media Service distributes only approved assets. Delivery evidence records
publication, reach, completion, and correction, allowing Billing and Payment to reconcile the
contract without exposing private athlete data.

7 Scores, Discovery, and Fairness
No single athlete score. MASAREN should not compress performance, health, character,
popularity, and commercial value into one number. The platform instead maintains explainable
dimensions: verified performance, consistency, development velocity, availability, competition
strength, training reliability, teamwork observations, integrity status, public impact, audience

8

engagement, and commercial readiness. Each dimension has a purpose, source boundary, confidence, validity period, and appeal mechanism.
Talent discovery. A scout specifies the role and evidence needed, such as age band, position, dominant foot, verified minutes, recent workload, technical observations, geographic availability, and consent. The matching engine first applies mandatory eligibility and then ranks
candidates using transparent weights. It displays missing data and confidence intervals so a
well-documented athlete is not automatically mistaken for a better athlete.
Institution confidence. Club or academy confidence reflects evidence completeness, verified
coaching credentials, safeguarding controls, result integrity, dispute handling, payment reliability, and data-quality responsiveness. It must not reward institutional size or social-media
volume by default. Public bodies and families receive the explanation and supporting evidence
rather than a badge with no accountable owner.
Integrity and correction. Suspicious statistics, duplicate media, identity conflicts, manipulated tests, betting-related concerns, or result disputes create cases for authorized owners. The
platform preserves original and corrected records, records the decision and evidence, and issues
compensating events to downstream profiles. Anti-doping and safeguarding cases remain governed by their specialist processes and privacy standards (International Olympic Committee,
2024; World Anti-Doping Agency, 2021a, 2021b).

8 Revenue and Ecosystem Value
Institution subscriptions. Federations, clubs, academies, schools, and event organizers can
subscribe to roster, competition, development, safeguarding, media, and reporting modules.
Pricing should scale with active participants, competitions, storage, and service level rather
than charging athletes for basic ownership of their verified records.
Opportunity transactions. MASAREN may charge transparent marketplace or workflow
fees for trials, camps, scholarships, scouting projects, sponsorships, or media rights when a
transaction is completed. The fee basis, payer, refund policy, and evidence of delivery must be
visible before acceptance.
Sponsor and broadcaster value. Sponsors receive verified inventory, consent status, audience segments, delivery evidence, brand-safety rules, and outcome reporting. Broadcasters
receive authoritative biographies, results, rights windows, highlights, and contextual story packages. Athletes and institutions retain approval and compensation rights; popularity signals can
improve discovery but cannot silently override safeguarding, rights, or editorial rules.
Public value. Aggregated reporting supports national participation strategy, youth pathways,
women’s sport, para-sport, facility investment, regional access, coach development, and program
accountability. The platform should publish methodology and coverage gaps so a lack of digitized data is not misread as a lack of sporting activity.

9 Governance, Delivery, and Acceptance
Decision rights. Federations own oﬀicial eligibility and results within their remit; clubs own
rosters and internal development records; athletes own consent and public visibility; clinical
and safeguarding professionals own restricted decisions; competition organizers own event operations; rights holders own media permissions; and MASAREN owns platform controls, auditability, contract enforcement, and derived-score methodology. Disputes route to the authoritative
owner with an escalation and appeal path.
Safeguarding and minors. Minor profiles are private by default, guardian authority is verified, direct contact is mediated, precise location is hidden, public media requires explicit approval, and suspicious contact or content is escalated. Safeguarding oﬀicers receive dedicated
workflows, immutable access logs, and confidentiality controls aligned with IOC guidance (International Olympic Committee, 2024).
Delivery phases. The foundation phase implements identity, institution, federation registration, athlete passport, consent, and audit. The pilot phase adds one sport, one federation or
league, selected clubs and academies, oﬀicial competition ingestion, and a limited scouting workflow. The scale phase adds multi-sport schemas, media and sponsorship rights, public-sector
dashboards, and cross-institution portability. Each phase exits only after data-owner sign-off,
security testing, athlete and guardian usability testing, recovery exercises, score validation, and
documented correction workflows.
Acceptance scenario. A complete pilot must onboard an institution, register an athlete,
verify guardian or athlete consent, issue eligibility, record a competition result, update the public
passport, restrict private welfare data, match an opportunity, approve media usage, reconcile a
sponsor deliverable, and reconstruct the complete history from audit and event records. Failure
at any downstream projection must be recoverable without changing the authoritative identity,
result, rights, or ledger record.
