Profiles & Multiple Identities

One of MASAREEN's most important design decisions is that identity is plural by default. A human being is never just one thing, and an organization is never just one type. The platform is built to model that reality instead of forcing everyone into a single box.

Individuals are not monoliths​

When you register (POST /api/v1/users/register) you pick a base audience — PROFESSIONAL or VOLUNTEER — and a role such as INDIVIDUAL. But that is only your starting identity. Over time you accumulate multiple profile identities, each with its own context:

  • Volunteer identity — skills, causes (SDGs), hours, verified tasks.
  • Professional / employee identity — linked to an organization, with role and responsibilities.
  • Donor / supporter identity — contributions and the impact they unlocked.
  • Member identity — belonging to programs, clubs, or communities.

These are not separate accounts. They are facets of one verified person, stitched together by your user record (GET /api/v1/users/me) and your reputation. You switch context the way you switch hats — and each hat carries its own proof.

Organizations are not monoliths either​

An entity that registers as an organization (POST /api/v1/organizations/) may simultaneously be:

  • An NGO running field programs.
  • A Government agency publishing public mandates.
  • A Company executing CSR/ESG commitments.
  • A Partner in collaborations (/api/v1/partners/).

The organization profile (PATCH /api/v1/organizations/{id}/profile) and its OII (Organization Impact Index) reflect all of these facets. Verification (POST /api/v1/organizations/{id}/verify) attests the legal/organizational identity so that opportunities it posts carry weight.

Why multiple identities matter​

  • Context-appropriate trust. A government reviewer and a volunteer teammate see the facets relevant to them — not your entire life.
  • No lost reputation. Switching from "volunteer at NGO A" to "employee at Company B" doesn't erase your PMS; it enriches it.
  • Cleaner matching. The matching service can match the right identity to the right opportunity (e.g., your "trainer" skills to a training task).

Identity data model (simplified)​

User (one login, one JWT subject)
 ├─ Identity: INDIVIDUAL / PROFESSIONAL / VOLUNTEER ...
 ├─ Profile  (name, nationality, avatar, bio)
 ├─ Skills   (POST /api/v1/users/me/skills)
 ├─ Causes   (SDG tags)
 ├─ Memberships (orgs, programs)
 ├─ Reputation (PMS, OII components)
 └─ Impact passport (portable verified record)
        │
Organization (legal entity)
 ├─ Type flags (NGO / GOV / COMPANY / PARTNER)
 ├─ Profile & verification
 ├─ Members (POST /api/v1/organizations/{id}/members)
 └─ OII (Organization Impact Index)

Privacy & control​

Identities are scoped. You control what each facet exposes, and the authorization service (/api/v1/authz/check) enforces who can see what. A follower sees a public profile (GET /api/v1/users/{id}); an auditor sees verification context; a stranger sees a portfolio summary.

The takeaway: MASAREEN does not ask "who are you?" once. It asks "which of your verified selves is relevant here?" — and that is what makes trust both granular and durable.
