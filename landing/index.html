The National Challenge

Activity is abundant. Evidence is fragmented.

Egypt's development ecosystem has real energy across ministries, universities, NGOs and the private sector, but achievements stay scattered across spreadsheets, social posts and manual reports.

Siloed Initiatives

Programs run independently, with no unified national view of development activity.

Weak Outcome Measurement

Success is counted in budget spent or attendance, not in real social return.

Manual Reporting Burden

Narrative reports and photo folders in inconsistent formats delay decisions.

Disconnected Youth Records

Volunteering, training and civic work leave no cumulative, verified record for employability.

Evidence Credibility Gaps

Claims lack auditable evidence trails, lowering public and international confidence.

No Interoperability

LMS, volunteer tools and grant portals were never built to answer cumulative national impact.

How It Works

From field action to attested impact.

Activity says what was done. Attestation proves what was achieved, where, by whom, and with what evidence.

Core principle: if something cannot be verified, it cannot become a trusted national record.

    Governance & Trust

    Sovereign by design. Accountable by default.

    Technology is not the main constraint, governance is. MASAREEN is built around privacy, human authority over AI, and open standards.

    Privacy & Security

    • Privacy by design: data minimization, purpose limitation and recorded consent
    • Zero-trust security with encryption, MFA and continuous monitoring
    • Tamper-evident evidence: hashes, chain of custody, immutable audit logs
    • Sovereign hosting options with encrypted backups and disaster recovery

    Responsible AI & Openness

    • AI assists; humans keep final authority on funding, reputation and recognition
    • Explainable scoring, published methodology, bias audits and appeal rights
    • Open APIs and data portability, no vendor lock-in
    • Arabic-first, low-bandwidth and offline-capable for inclusion

    Implementation Roadmap

    From mandate to national deployment.

    A staged, evidence-gated 36-month path. No national rollout before the pilot proves that evidence can be captured, verified, trusted and reused.

    P0

    Months 0–3 · Mandate & Design

    Ministerial mandate, steering council, legal review, data governance charter, pilot sectors, architecture and procurement plan.

    P1

    Months 4–12 · Sovereign Pilot

    Agriculture/rural development and youth programs in 3–5 governorates: identity prototype, field app, evidence vault, initial dashboards and independent audit.

    P2

    Months 13–24 · Multi-Sector Expansion

    Education, environment, health and CSR modules; 15-governorate expansion; marketplace beta; media evidence desk.

    P3

    Months 25–36 · National Deployment

    Nationwide rollout, full command center, SDG reporting automation, interoperability APIs and a national communication program.

    P4

    Beyond Month 36 · Regional Leadership

    Exportable model, regional partnerships, Arabic impact standards and international donor interoperability.

    Honest answers

    Questions people ask first.

    Is MASAREEN live today?

    No. It is a pilot proposal (June 2026). The feasibility study recommends a 12-month sovereign pilot in agriculture/rural development and youth empowerment, with stop/go gates, before any national rollout. Numbers on this page are targets, not results.

    Is this a surveillance system?

    It is designed not to be. Data is minimized, consent is recorded, people can correct or export their records, and sensitive evidence has restricted access. The study lists “not a surveillance system” as an explicit design requirement.

    Does AI decide who gets recognized or funded?

    No. AI flags missing evidence, duplicates and unusual patterns. For funding, reputation, jobs and public recognition, a human makes the final call, scoring is explainable, and people can appeal.

    Who owns the data?

    The proposal is sovereign: Egypt owns the platform logic, impact taxonomy, evidence standards and dashboards, hosted on national or regionally controlled infrastructure, with open APIs and data portability so there is no vendor lock-in. Individuals control what their passport shares.

    What about people with weak internet or no smartphone?

    Arabic-first design, low-bandwidth mode, offline capture with later sync, assisted submission through universities, youth centers and NGOs, and non-digital fallbacks, so exclusion doesn't become a hidden cost.

    How can my organization get involved?

    Send us a short note using the form below. Pilot partners are expected to include a lead ministry, selected governorates, universities, NGOs, CSR partners and independent verifiers.
